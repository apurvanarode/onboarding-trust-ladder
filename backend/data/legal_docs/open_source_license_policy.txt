OPEN SOURCE SOFTWARE LICENSE POLICY

Acme Systems, Inc.
Document Reference: POLICY-OSS-003
Version: 1.4 | Status: Active

This policy governs the evaluation, approval, and use of open source software (OSS) dependencies
within all products and internal systems developed or maintained by Acme Systems, Inc.

1. SCOPE
   This policy applies to all software engineers, data engineers, and technical contractors who
   introduce new third-party libraries or open source packages into any Acme Systems codebase,
   regardless of programming language or platform.

2. LICENSE-CHECK REQUIREMENT
   No new open source dependency may be added to any Acme Systems project without first completing
   the License Approval Process. Engineers must submit a License Review Request via the internal
   developer portal prior to merging any pull request that introduces a new dependency.

3. PRE-APPROVED LICENSES
   The following licenses are pre-approved and do not require individual review:
   (a) MIT License — permitted without restriction for commercial use.
   (b) Apache License 2.0 — permitted without restriction; engineers must retain the NOTICE
       file where required by the license terms.

4. LICENSES REQUIRING MANAGER APPROVAL
   The following licenses require explicit written approval from the engineer's direct manager
   before use, due to potential copyleft obligations:
   (a) GNU General Public License (GPL) v2 and v3 — use in any product distributed externally
       must be reviewed by the Legal team in addition to manager approval.

5. PROHIBITED LICENSES
   Any license not listed in clauses 3 or 4 is considered unapproved by default and must not
   be used until a formal review has been completed and recorded.

6. AUDIT AND COMPLIANCE
   Acme Systems conducts quarterly automated license scans across all repositories. Any
   unapproved dependency discovered during an audit must be remediated within ten (10)
   business days or the affected component must be taken offline until resolution.

7. RESPONSIBILITY
   The engineer who merges a dependency is responsible for ensuring license compliance.
   Ignorance of a dependency's license terms is not an accepted defence in an audit finding.
